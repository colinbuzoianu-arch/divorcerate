Here's one of the most hopeful findings in relationship research: it's not whether you fight that predicts how a relationship goes, and it's not even how often. It's how well you recover afterwards. The technical name for that recovery skill is the **repair attempt**, and couples who are good at it are remarkably resilient — even when they argue a lot.

## What is a repair attempt?

A repair attempt is any gesture — a word, a joke, a touch, a softening — that tries to de-escalate tension and reconnect during or after conflict. The relationship researcher John Gottman called repair attempts "the secret weapon of emotionally intelligent couples."

They can be tiny and unglamorous:

- "Can we start over? That came out wrong."
- "I need a hug."
- A bit of shared humour that breaks the tension.
- "I hear you. Let me try again."
- Simply reaching for your partner's hand.

What makes them work isn't elegance — it's that they interrupt the downward spiral and say, underneath, *we're still on the same team.*

## Why they matter so much

Every couple accumulates friction. The difference between couples who thrive and couples who slowly corrode isn't the absence of friction — it's whether they can metabolise it. Repair attempts are how you do that. They stop a bad moment from becoming a bad day, and a bad day from becoming a pile of unrepaired resentment (the kind of pile that eventually turns into [contempt](/blog/contempt-in-relationships)).

This is why, in our [relationship test](/relationship-test), conflict and repair is one of the most heavily weighted of the eight dimensions. Fast, reliable repair is one of the strongest protective factors a relationship can have.

## Why repair sometimes fails

Repair attempts have two halves: making them, and *receiving* them. Often the attempt is there, but it doesn't land — because one partner is still too flooded to notice it, or too hurt to accept it.

That's worth knowing, because it reframes a lot of stuck arguments. If your partner cracks a small joke or says "okay, okay" and you're too angry to take the olive branch, the repair fails — not because they didn't try, but because the attempt couldn't get through. Learning to *accept* a clumsy repair is as important as making a graceful one.

## How to get better at it

1. **Lower the bar for making them.** A repair attempt doesn't have to be a well-crafted apology. "This is getting heated, can we slow down?" is enough.
2. **Agree on a signal.** Some couples pick a phrase or even a silly word that means "I'm reaching for you, please take my hand." Having one ready makes repair possible even mid-argument.
3. **Watch for flooding.** When either of you is too physiologically overwhelmed to think straight, no repair will land. Take a real 20-minute break and come back — then try again. (Shutting down and vanishing without that signal is [stonewalling](/blog/stonewalling); the break only works if you say you're taking one and genuinely return.)
4. **Receive generously.** When your partner reaches for you, even awkwardly, try to take the hand. You can always finish the conversation once you're both calmer.

## When to get help

If you keep trying to repair and it never seems to land — if every attempt gets swatted away, or one of you consistently shuts down — that loop is exactly the kind of thing couples therapy is good at interrupting.
