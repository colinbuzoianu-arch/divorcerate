Everything else is just particles.

A short essay on the strangest thing in the known universe, and on what it takes to keep it.

Scroll

01 · The cold

The universe is enormous, ancient and almost completely indifferent.

Out there, matter drifts or collapses. Gas clouds condense into stars, and stars burn out, explode or fall into black holes. Forces pull and push, but nothing reaches out. Nothing waits up for anyone to come home.

02 · The exception

Then, on one small planet, matter began to notice other matter.

As far as we know, Earth is the only place where pieces of the universe look at each other and care what they see. Where two of them laugh at the same joke. Where one stays awake because the other can’t sleep. We haven’t searched everywhere. But everywhere we have looked, this is rare.

03 · Same origin

You and the person next to you were made in the same old stars.

Almost every atom in your body heavier than hydrogen and helium was forged inside stars that died before the Sun existed. That includes the calcium in your bones and the iron in your blood. When two people find each other, it is, quite literally, the universe meeting itself again.

04 · Binding

Gravity pulls. Love chooses.

Physics has forces that bind: gravity, electromagnetism, and the strong force inside every atomic nucleus. They don’t need a reason. Love is the only binding we know of that has to be chosen, and chosen again, on ordinary Tuesdays.

Gravity

Holds planets to stars and stars to galaxies.

Needs no choice

Electromagnetism

Holds electrons to nuclei, and atoms to each other.

Needs no choice

Love

Holds two people who could each walk away, and don’t.

Chosen, again and again

05 · Entropy

Left alone, everything drifts toward disorder. Relationships too.

Destruction is everywhere in space, and it needs no help. Hate and contempt are the same force turned toward people. They break the bond, and they wear down the person who carries them.

Horseman 1

Criticism

Attacking who someone is, rather than what they did.

Horseman 2 · the strongest predictor

Contempt

Sarcasm, eye-rolling, mockery. Speaking from above.

Horseman 3

Defensiveness

Never being the one at fault. Turning every complaint back.

Horseman 4

Stonewalling

Shutting down and going silent until the other gives up.

From decades of research on couples by psychologist John Gottman, who found these four patterns predict relationships coming apart, with contempt the strongest single sign.

06 · Keeping it warm

Warmth isn’t kept by grand gestures. It’s kept by small turnings.

5:1

Stable couples, Gottman’s research found, have roughly five positive interactions for every negative one, even while they argue.

  1. i

    Turn toward the small bids

    “Look at this.” “Guess what happened.” These tiny reaches for attention are where closeness is built or lost.

  2. ii

    Repair early

    A joke, a touch, an “I’m sorry, let me try again”, before the argument hardens.

  3. iii

    Stay curious

    Keep learning who your partner is becoming, not who they were when you met.

07 · What we make

Everything people make carries a little of this.

Songs, letters, gardens, cities, even software. We build things because someone, somewhere, mattered to us. This site exists for the same reason: two people deserve an honest, gentle look at what holds them together.
